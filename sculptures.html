Sculpture · Installation

Shape in Space

Totems in steel, bronze and 24k gold, cut aluminum in car paint, and a school bus.

The work

Drawing in space

This section summarises an essay on the sculptures by John K. Grande, writer and art critic.

The cut-aluminum sculptures have a post-Pop flair and a debt to Frank Stella. Shaped metal is finished in car paint, and the forms sit at different depths, so the gaps between them matter as much as the shapes.

Lines curve almost in parallel, one behind another, held by oversized fixings that look like rivets. The effect is theatrical: a machine that works only in the imagination. The shapes cut for one large piece leave negative offcuts, and those are reused in another, so the two works speak to each other about presence and absence.

Smaller, more recent sculptures begin with paint drips from the studio floor. They rise from painted blocks and carry something of Arp's surrealist forms. The critic also compares the organic shapes to Frederick Kiesler's experiments with biomorphic design.

The language is neither abstract nor figurative. It is about perception: how colour and shape build meaning, and how an object in space changes the way we read the space around it.

Installation · 2013

Incident

A school bus shot through with bullet holes. It premiered at Nuit Blanche in Toronto, then toured to Washington, D.C. in April 2013 during the U.S. gun debates. It is in the permanent collection of Artisan Works in Rochester, New York.

Violence as beauty

The story of Incident

Bus photographs © John Drajewicz. Select any photograph to enlarge it.

  • 2012

    The ideaWith Toronto's Nuit Blanche approaching, Mitic wanted to take the bullet works somewhere new. He bought a yellow school bus and fired about 6,000 rounds into it. He named it Incident and thought of it as forensic evidence: a study of how closely violence and beauty now sit together.

  • 2012

    Nuit BlancheThe bus was moved to Todmorden Mills for the festival. Parents who saw it beforehand called the police, and the organisers asked that it stay covered until the night itself. It was on view for seven hours before he was asked to remove it.

  • 2013

    To WashingtonAfter the Newtown school shooting, talks with peers led to a U.S. tour. Charles Krause, a Washington gallery owner and journalist, invited the bus to his gallery event The Newtown Project: Art Targets Guns.

  • 2013

    Around the cityLoaded onto a flatbed truck, Incident was parked and shown near the White House, in Georgetown, in Chinatown, in Adams Morgan and at George Mason University.

  • 2013

    CoverageFox News, CBS, ABC, MSNBC, The Washington Post and the Huffington Post ran feature stories, many with interviews. In Canada it was covered by the CBC, CTV, Global and the Toronto Star.

  • Since

    AcquiredAn American collector in New York State acquired the bus, and it remains there.
