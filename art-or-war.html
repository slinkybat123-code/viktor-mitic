Series · Bullet-hole paintings

Art or War

Portraits and icons, painted and then outlined with live ammunition.

The idea

Can a gun make something?

“Guns were made to destroy, but can they be used to create something beautiful?”Viktor Mitic

Mitic starts from a history of sacred places and symbols being attacked. He points to the giant Buddhas of Bamiyan, destroyed in 2001, and to twelfth-century frescoes in Kosovo defaced by gunfire.

His answer is to turn the weapon around. He believes the impulses to create and to destroy sit close together, an idea he traces to the Upanishads, and he uses one to do the work of the other.

He paints the image first, then outlines it with bullets. A friend, a writer, coined the name for the result: bullet hole paintings. The subjects include Gandhi, John F. Kennedy and John Lennon, whose lives were ended by guns, as well as Jesus, Marilyn Monroe, John Wayne and Chairman Mao. The Lennon portrait was made with the same model of gun that killed him.

Mitic has said he does not think guns should exist. He does not call himself an activist, and leaves it to the viewer to decide what to do about them.
