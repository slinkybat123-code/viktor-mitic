The Artist

Creation, close to destruction.

Viktor Mitic is a Serbian-born Canadian painter and sculptor based in Toronto.

He grew up in the former Yugoslavia, where national service taught him to handle firearms, and later moved to Canada. He holds a BFA from the University of Toronto and a Fine Arts Diploma from Sheridan College.

His practice runs in several directions at once. The Rain Dance paintings are made outdoors and left to the weather. The Art or War paintings are outlined in bullet holes, a way of turning a tool built for harm into one that makes an image. His sculptures are cut from aluminum and finished in car paint.

Mitic says the gun works are meant to start a conversation about gun violence, and he leaves the conclusions to the viewer.

Series · Painting

Rain Dance

See the full series

Every painting in the series was made outside, in the rain. Natural elements mix with traditional painting mediums, so the weather on the day decides part of the picture.

Mitic found the method by accident and calls it acid rain painting. Galaxy (2008), first shown at Muramatsu Gallery in Tokyo, is on the cover of the Rain Dance book.

Series · Bullet-hole paintings

Art or War

Mitic paints the image, then outlines it with gunfire. His subjects include Gandhi, John F. Kennedy and John Lennon, people whose lives were ended by guns, alongside Jesus, Marilyn Monroe and Chairman Mao.

Hole Jesus (2008) was made with .22-calibre rounds. Blasted Beaverbrook (2009) was commissioned by the Beaverbrook Art Gallery in New Brunswick. Blasted Guernica, his answer to Picasso, is in the collection of the Remington Contemporary Art Gallery in Markham, Ontario.

Installation · 2013

Incident

A school bus riddled with bullet holes, shown in Toronto and in Washington, D.C., where it arrived as a rolling exhibit against gun violence. It is now in the permanent collection of Artisan Works in Rochester, New York.

Sculpture & installation

Shape in space

All sculptures

The sculptures are cut from aluminum and sprayed in car paint, with a post-Pop energy. Shapes are layered at different depths so that a piece reads as a drawing in space.

The critic John K. Grande has written on the series. Smaller works grow from paint drips in the studio. The largest, Hypervelocity Asteroid (2016), measures 22 × 3 × 10 feet and was acquired by the Elizabeth Collection in Rochester, New York.

Selected history

1995 — 2017
  • 1995

    Paints a portrait of Prime Minister Jean Chrétien, unveiled at the prime minister's office in Ottawa.

  • 2008

    Hole Jesus is shown at Trias Gallery in Toronto. Galaxy is shown at Muramatsu Gallery in Tokyo.

  • 2009

    The Beaverbrook Art Gallery commissions Blasted Beaverbrook. Dealers, 36 portraits of Toronto's art dealers, opens at Odon Wagner Contemporary.

  • 2010

    Art or War opens at Gallery Moos in Toronto, with a book and a short film of the same name.

  • 2011

    Fourfront Editions publishes Rain Dance as a book and film.

  • 2013

    Incident is installed in Washington, D.C.

  • 2014

    The documentary Gun Porn, on how the bullet paintings are made, screens at Hot Docs.

  • 2015

    Artisan Works in Rochester, New York, exhibits the 58 works it acquired.

  • 2016

    Blasted Guernica goes on show in the permanent collection of the Remington Contemporary Art Gallery in Markham, Ontario.

  • 2017

    The Elizabeth Collection in Rochester acquires Hypervelocity Asteroid.
