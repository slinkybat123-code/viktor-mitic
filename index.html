The Artist

Creation, close to destruction.

Viktor Mitic is a Serbian-born Canadian painter and sculptor with studios in Toronto and Sarasota, Florida.

He grew up in the former Yugoslavia, where national service taught him to handle firearms, and later moved to Canada. He holds a Bachelor of Fine Art from the University of Toronto and a Fine Arts Diploma from Sheridan College.

His practice runs in several directions at once. The Rain Dance paintings are made outdoors and left to the weather. The Art or War paintings are outlined in bullet holes, a way of turning a tool built for harm into one that makes an image. His sculptures range from cut aluminum finished in car paint to stainless steel plated in 24k gold.

Mitic says the gun works are meant to start a conversation about gun violence, and he leaves the conclusions to the viewer.

Series · Painting

Rain Dance

See the full series

Every painting in the series was made outside, in the rain. Natural elements mix with traditional painting mediums, so the weather on the day decides part of the picture.

Mitic found the method by accident and calls it acid rain painting. Galaxy (2008), first shown at Muramatsu Gallery in Tokyo, is on the cover of the Rain Dance book.

Series · Bullet-hole paintings

Art or War

Mitic paints the image, then outlines it with gunfire. His subjects include Gandhi, John F. Kennedy and John Lennon, people whose lives were ended by guns, alongside Jesus, Marilyn Monroe and Chairman Mao.

Hole Jesus (2008) was made with .22-calibre rounds. Blasted Beaverbrook (2009) was commissioned by the Beaverbrook Art Gallery in New Brunswick. Blasted Guernica, his answer to Picasso, is on permanent view at the Remington Contemporary Art Museum in Markham, Ontario.

Installation · 2013

Incident

A school bus riddled with bullet holes. It premiered at Nuit Blanche in Toronto and toured to Washington, D.C. in April 2013, at the height of the U.S. gun debates. It is now in the permanent collection of Artisan Works in Rochester, New York.

Sculpture & installation

Shape in space

All sculptures

The early sculptures are cut from aluminum and sprayed in car paint, with a post-Pop energy. Recent pieces grow from paint splatters found on the studio floor and become totems in stainless steel, bronze and 24k gold, some of them 16 feet tall.

They now stand in public and private collections from Port Moody, British Columbia, to Doha and Colombo.

Selected history

Full CV
  • 1995

    Paints a portrait of Prime Minister Jean Chrétien, unveiled at the prime minister's office in Ottawa.

  • 2008

    Hole Jesus is shown at Trias Gallery in Toronto. Galaxy is shown at Muramatsu Gallery in Tokyo.

  • 2009

    The Beaverbrook Art Gallery commissions Blasted Beaverbrook. Dealers, 36 portraits of Toronto's art dealers, opens at Odon Wagner Contemporary.

  • 2010

    Art or War opens at Gallery Moos in Toronto, with a book and a short film. The work travels to the Armory Show in New York and Art Chicago.

  • 2011

    Fourfront Editions publishes Rain Dance as a book and film.

  • 2013

    Incident tours to Washington, D.C. during the U.S. gun debates.

  • 2014

    The documentary Gun Porn, on how the bullet paintings are made, screens at Hot Docs.

  • 2015

    Artisan Works in Rochester, New York, exhibits the 58 works it acquired. Blasted Guernica goes on permanent view in Markham, Ontario.

  • 2018

    Lyric Star and The Music Garage are installed at the Marriott Hotel in Markham. The Biggs Museum of American Art adds a work to its collection.

  • 2021

    Heartland becomes public art in Port Moody, British Columbia.

  • 2023

    Sculptures enter private collections in Sonoma and New Jersey. A second studio is working in Sarasota, Florida.
