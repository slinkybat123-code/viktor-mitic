News · February 3, 2023

New collectors

Viktor Mitic's sculptures have entered private, public and commercial collections in California, British Columbia and New Jersey.

The announcement

Sculptures find new homes

Summary of a press release issued from Sarasota, Florida, and Toronto, Ontario.

Collectors have been acquiring Mitic's sculptures for homes, public spaces and commercial projects, sometimes two pieces at a time. He is best known for the earlier bullet paintings, and the sculptures show how far the work has moved since.

During the pandemic he set up a second studio in Sarasota, Florida, alongside Toronto. The release names Oeno Gallery, in Prince Edward County, Ontario, as the gallery representing his work. The gallery describes the recent sculptures as paint splatters from the studio floor turned into tall, colourful totems.

The forms are neither abstract nor figurative. Some stand 16 feet tall.

Who acquired what

  • Sonoma

    Celestial and BlackAcquired by Gary Spain, California interior designer and executive creative director at RH, for his private Sonoma estate. Celestial is 24k gold-plated stainless steel.

  • Port Moody

    HeartlandAcquired by the City of Port Moody, British Columbia, known as the City of the Arts. The 9 ft bronze and black sculpture stands in the centre of the community.

  • New Jersey

    Gold TotemAcquired for the Shoe-Inn Distribution Center in Englewood. The tall sculpture in 24k gold-plated stainless steel stands on a pedestal in the entry gallery.

  • Abroad

    Doha and ColomboInternational collectors now reach as far as Doha, Qatar, and Colombo, Sri Lanka.

Background

About the artist

Mitic's bullet-riddled paintings comment on attitudes to guns, violence and fame. Incident, the shot-out school bus, premiered at Nuit Blanche in Toronto and was shown in Washington, D.C. in April 2013.

His work has been exhibited at the Armory Show in New York, Gallery Moos in Toronto, Art Chicago, Muramatsu Gallery in Tokyo and the Beaverbrook Art Gallery. Earlier collectors include two former prime ministers of Canada, Stephen Harper and Jean Chrétien.
